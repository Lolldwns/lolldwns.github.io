Predicting therapy response

2024 – 2025MSc thesis · Imperial College London · Merit
Background

Gambling disorder has no validated biomarkers and no licensed treatments. My thesis combined normative PET receptor maps with resting-state fMRI from people with gambling disorder and healthy controls.

We found differences in 5-HT2A- and 5-HT1B-enriched connectivity in prefrontal and parietal regions as suspected diagnostic biomarkers. To test how well these novel biomarkers predict treatment outcome, I then applied a series of machine learning models using LOOCV, permutation testing, and feature importance analyses.

Dementia sub-typing ↗

2024 – 2025MSc · Machine Learning in Neuroscience · Imperial College London
Background

I trained a model on a multimodal dataset combining biological and behavioural measures to predict agitation in people with dementia. Hourly readings from home activity sensors, heart rate, blood pressure, breathing and sleep were aggregated into daily patterns, and the model classified whether a day was likely to include an agitation episode.

The project was an assessment as part of our Machine Learning in Neuroscience course at Imperial. My project ranked #1 in the MSc cohort as the highest-performing model.

Approach

Missing physiological data was imputed, outliers handled and features scaled, then ranked and selected by Random Forest importance, with SMOTE to balance the classes. I compared six models with k-fold cross-validation and a patient-level split, and K-nearest neighbours performed best.

Classifying Pilates form ↗

2025Personal project · Deep learning
Background

A home project to assess Pilates form automatically with deep learning. I chose it naturally because I lived with my sister at the time, who is a trained Pilates instructor. We recorded lots of footage of us both doing the single-leg stretch, and she labelled each recording as good or bad form, giving a balanced dataset.

Pipeline

MediaPipe extracts 18 features per frame: joint angles, body symmetry, alignment and leg/toe straightness. These feed a bidirectional LSTM in PyTorch with input normalisation, attention and confidence scoring, trained with binary cross-entropy and early stopping, and evaluated on a held-out test set.

87%accuracy on unseen data
0.94AUC-ROC

A proof of concept: it won't generalise to other moves or more varied people yet, but could be extended with joint-level error feedback, a more diverse dataset, and transfer learning to new exercises.

Hsp90 + LA1011

2020 – 2023BSc dissertation · University of Sussex · First Class Honours
Background

Hsp90 is a chaperone protein that helps tau aggregate in Alzheimer's disease. LA1011, a new dihydropyridine derivative, binds Hsp90 and could be a neuroprotective therapeutic.

I experimentally determined the structure of Hsp90 in complex with LA1011. I used a series of ITC experiments, including the co-chaperone FKBP51, to measure binding strength and competition, and crystallography to visualise where the drug binds, giving a starting point for future structural changes.
